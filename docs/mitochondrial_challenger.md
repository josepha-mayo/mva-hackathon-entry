# A second route: preserve useful neural output under chromosome instability

Public-source research decision, 23 October 2026. **Elamipretide is nominated for a bounded mitochondrial-function experiment, not as a superior treatment.** It does not replace ataluren on clinical evidence, and no tested medicine has demonstrated rescue of the proposed BUB1B genotype. The hypothesis is that chromosome instability causes a secondary mitochondrial constraint that remains modifiable after a functional deficit appears. Neither that constraint nor a drug response is established in this child.

## Why this is more specific than another antioxidant list

A 2026 study depleted **bub3 or rod in developing fly neural stem cells**. Complex aneuploidies accumulated, accompanied by proteostasis and mitochondrial defects. Genetic Hsp60/Hsp60c or Sod2/GTPx-1 overexpression improved progeny counts and restored brain size. These were genetic interventions in another checkpoint model, not an elamipretide experiment or evidence of restored neural computation. Brain size could also recover after apoptosis suppression, which makes size alone an especially poor success criterion. [González-Blanco et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-70521-0)

The same paper found that autophagy-enhancing genetic interventions improved stem-cell counts without restoring progeny or brain size. Dietary rapamycin also improved stem-cell counts; the text does not justify assigning every downstream genetic result to the drug. This separates **retaining precursors** from **producing useful descendants**. A blanket instruction to increase autophagy, suppress apoptosis or lower ROS would miss that distinction.

Elamipretide targets mitochondrial cardiolipin. An actual-drug study in aged mouse oocytes reported improved fertilization and blastocyst development and reduced aneuploidy at 10 micromolar. This is useful functional evidence beyond a fluorescence marker, but it concerns meiosis and aging, not ongoing BUB1B-defective neural mitosis. [January 2026 oocyte study](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13048-026-01975-5)

## Evidence that prevents promotion to a treatment lead

The FDA approved Forzinity in September 2025 for Barth syndrome in patients weighing at least 30 kg, under accelerated approval based on an intermediate muscle-strength endpoint. In the small randomized crossover trial, the walking and fatigue primary endpoints were not superior to placebo; longer open-label strength findings supported approval. The larger MMPOWER-3 mitochondrial-myopathy trial also failed its primary endpoints. Its exploratory nuclear-DNA subgroup signal remains hypothesis-generating. These are substantive human negatives, alongside a real marketed product. [FDA trial snapshot](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-trials-snapshots/drug-trials-snapshots-forzinity), [MMPOWER-3](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000207402)

Plasma exposure cannot be substituted for neural mitochondrial exposure. The Friedreich-ataxia protocol describes limited blood-brain-barrier penetration. Published cortical-homogenate detection and mouse cognitive positives do not establish free drug inside human neurons. A separate long-term aged-mouse study improved a cognitive measure in females but not males. The positive oocyte concentration is not a qualified brain concentration. [Trial protocol record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05168774), [mouse brain detection](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddx052), [neurovascular positive](https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.12731), [sex-dependent cognitive result](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9562127/)

The same elamipretide registry now has posted results, not just a protocol: 16 participants started treatment, 13 supplied week-52 primary visual-acuity data, and eight completed all visits. Median high-contrast change was +5.5 letters in the lower-exposure group (n=6) and 0 in the higher group (n=7). There was no placebo group or posted comparative significance test. Lack-of-efficacy and adverse-event withdrawals are recorded. This mixed, incomplete study neither establishes a functional benefit nor proves failed neural delivery. [Posted results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05168774?tab=results)

The completed NuPOWER study (NCT05162768; 102 enrolled) has no registry results posted at this review. A May 2026 sponsor announcement reports genotype-dependent POLG1-subgroup evidence and a conference abstract, without quantitative overall outcomes. This is a potentially relevant update, not verified overall success or failure and not BUB1B evidence. [NuPOWER registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05162768), [sponsor conference announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/stealth-biotherapeutics-to-present-updates-on-elamipretide-in-barth-syndrome-and-polg-disease-at-euromit-2026-302779660.html)

**Omaveloxolone is a serious rival, not an interchangeable substitute.** MOXIe showed a neurological primary-endpoint benefit in Friedreich ataxia (103 randomized; 82 in the primary analysis), while MOTOR found no primary peak-work or secondary walking benefit in mitochondrial myopathy. [MOXIe](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.25934), [MOTOR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7176297/)

At 50 nM, a 2026 rat sensory-neuron study found survival and respiration benefits after frataxin knockdown. Treatment began before the reported protein decline and largely prevented frataxin loss in the responsive condition; survival improvement was significant there but not in the more severe knockdown. The result is not a pure downstream-support demonstration. It does not demonstrate recovery after established loss or BUB1B rescue. [Primary DRG study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopha.2026.119031)

Omaveloxolone also inhibited LONP1 and induced mitochondrial aggregation in susceptible leukemia models, while sparing tested normal hematopoietic controls. That is a mechanism-specific warning for a proteostasis hypothesis, not proof of neural toxicity. Protective and inhibitory exposure windows must be resolved in the target cells. Its 97% plasma binding further prevents treating nominal culture concentration as free human exposure. [JCI primary study](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI196687), [EMA product information](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/product-information/skyclarys-epar-product-information_en.pdf)

## A finite experiment that can reject the new route

Use an available renewable neural model carrying the relevant genotype and an explicitly identified corrective/process reference. Establish baseline function and assay competence alongside the pilot. **For a recovery claim, document the deficit before adding drug.** Do not assume a stop-only corrected WT/N1002K line is fully normal.

One initial module has **two genotypes by four conditions: vehicle, two elamipretide exposures and one NAC comparator**. This is eight assigned conditions before biological replication. It can replace the ciliary/barrier module when the neural platform is more relevant and ready; it is not an obligatory addition to every assay. Select measured exposures and duration prospectively with the responsible scientists. No clinical dose, achievable neural concentration, power or numerical safety margin is established here.

Measure evoked neural function per well with survival/coverage and starting-cell accounting. In a precursor model, additionally quantify functional neuronal output per enrolled precursor and remaining precursor reserve. Keep total functional output and per-surviving-neuron function separate so selective loss cannot manufacture improvement. Respiration, ATP-linked output and mitochondrial stress are supporting mechanism measurements, not substitutes for neural function. Randomized wells are treatment units; cells and descendants are not independent donors.

Advance a reproducible hit only with useful function at an interpretable exposure, without explanatory cell loss, excessive bursting, precursor exhaustion or increased abnormal-lineage expansion. In dividing cultures, retain division-error and daughter-fate accounting and appropriate chromosome measurements. A postmitotic benefit need not repair every chromosome or induce proliferation. A negative result with competent controls closes the tested drug bridge; an unqualified exposure is unresolved, not proof of inefficacy.

## How the challenger competes

| Route | Its strongest distinct advantage | What must still be shown |
|---|---|---|
| Ataluren | Direct attempt to recover useful stop-derived BUBR1 | Native-context functional rescue and feasible exposure; band increase alone is inadequate. |
| Elamipretide | A distinct mitochondrial target with a disease-adjacent CIN rationale | Actual-drug neural function under BUB1B failure, plus neural exposure. |
| Lithium carbonate | Adult neuronal-circuit and memory recovery in another aneuploid mouse model. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI64650) | A useful response despite ongoing CIN; Wnt analogy and brain entry are insufficient. |
| Azithromycin | An alternative stop-suppression experiment. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00109-015-1364-1) | Native BUBR1 product and useful daughter output; another-gene protein signal is insufficient. |

These evidence types cannot be converted into one efficacy score. Elamipretide earns a **second, mechanistically distinct research comparison**, not a claim that it beats ataluren, NAC, lithium or a qualified tissue route. The accompanying reviews preserve the wider search and negative findings.
