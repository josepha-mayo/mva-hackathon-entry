# A correct-looking metaphase can still produce the wrong daughters

Joseph Mayo · MVA 2026 Track 2 · 23 October 2026

**Research decision:** the proposed N1002K comparison must distinguish
stabilizing correct chromosome attachments from stabilizing incorrect ones.
Preserved arrest, aligned chromosomes and cold-stable fibers are not jointly
sufficient to establish faithful segregation. None of the studies below
tested this exact allele or demonstrated a treatment for this child.

## The closest MVA evidence is a genetic experiment

Xu and colleagues reported two different experiments in 2013:

| Location | Model and intervention | Established endpoint and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Figure 3 | B56-depleted or B56-binding-defective BUBR1 replacement in HeLa cells; ZM447439, including 2 micromolar for one hour in MG132-containing medium | Improved congression and/or cold-stable attachment readouts under an imposed metaphase block; not completed faithful divisions |
| Figure 4 | Two established MVA fibroblast lines; expression of BUBR1 constructs or kinetochore-targeted MIS12-KARD-3D, with MG132 before fixation | Local B56 recruitment restored alignment; a genetic rescue of one endpoint, not an MVA drug-response experiment |

The figure-level distinction matters: the discussion's broad summary cannot
be used to turn the HeLa chemical experiment into a drug rescue in MVA cells.
The MVA genetic result strengthens the relevance of local recruitment while
crediting an existing tool. It does not establish native feedback, this
allele's lesion, normal anaphase or a therapeutic exposure window.
[Xu et al., 2013, Figures 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1242/bio.20134051)

## Two ways an apparently good checkpoint result can mislead

Ditchfield and colleagues found context-dependent checkpoint failure after
Aurora inhibition: a short-term nocodazole response could persist while the
paclitaxel response was compromised. Prolonged arrest changed the result.
Thus one spindle-disruption response cannot certify all checkpoint behavior;
normal mitotic exit timing did not establish successful division.
[Ditchfield et al., 2003, Figures 3–5](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.200208091)

There is a separate escape route even when the checkpoint works. A single
kinetochore attached toward both spindle poles can escape checkpoint
detection. The 2001 PtK1 study established this attachment geometry; the 2006
primary abstract reports increased lagging chromosomes after partial Aurora
inhibition despite little effect on alignment, histone phosphorylation and
cytokinesis. Those are other systems, not an exact-allele result. The 2006
finding was reviewed at abstract level here; its reported concentration is
not transferred as a protocol or exposure limit.
[Cimini et al., 2001](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.153.3.517),
[Cimini et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16950108/)

**Hypothesis refinement:** insufficient local phosphatase recruitment might
prevent useful attachments, while excessive stabilization might retain wrong
attachments. Restoring regulated recruitment must allow correct attachments
to persist and incorrect attachments to be removed before release. This is a
testable inference for the proposed allele, not proof of its mechanism.

## The experiment changes in three practical ways

1. Keep unchallenged divisions as the primary setting for benefit. In
   separate renewable-cell assay-development cohorts, qualify both an
   unattached-kinetochore checkpoint response and a microtubule-present
   attachment-error challenge. A monopolar-to-bipolar recovery comparison is
   one possible tool, subject to feasibility and correction controls. Score
   error resolution before anaphase, premature release, completion and
   daughter outcomes; fixed alignment after washout is insufficient. Do not
   interpret this as proof that the checkpoint directly senses tension.
2. Where feasible, resolve attachment geometry rather than infer correct
   biorientation from alignment or cold stability. If imaging cannot
   distinguish merotelic attachments, report that limit and require direct
   segregation/daughter-genome measurements. Lagging chromatin or micronuclei
   alone are not verified whole-chromosome karyotypes. The challenge can
   itself generate errors, so it cannot replace basal benefit measurements.
3. Qualify a published alignment-improving Aurora perturbation as a possible
   discordant assay benchmark, alongside correction and the controlled local
   recruitment bypass. Its expected behavior must be demonstrated in the
   chosen model; it is not a presumed positive or a repositioning lead.
   Maintain the same completion and daughter-fate requirements. Neither a
   global kinase inhibitor nor a maximum-strength phosphatase tether is
   presumed safe because a fixed image improves.

These are sibling assay cohorts, not a claim that all challenges should be
stacked onto one cell. Assay qualification must establish timing, usable
conditions and measurement error before confirmation. Independent edit
events/backgrounds remain the biological units. Already-normal functions
require preservation within justified, frozen margins. No new numerical
threshold or signed baseline rule is introduced here.

## Survival alone also fails the tissue test

In a 2023 developing-cortex BubR1 conditional-loss mouse study, Trp53
co-deletion reduced some apoptosis and partially preserved some progenitors,
but DNA damage persisted and later cortical rescue was limited. This is
direct counterevidence to assuming that keeping damaged cells alive restores
the tissue. It does not test this human allele or predict postnatal response.
[Sterling et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2023.1282182)

A separate 2025 neonatal hypoxic-injury study links restored BUBR1 expression
with STAT3-related responses in a different injury setting. Only its abstract
was available in this review. It does not justify a STAT3 drug, a neural
regeneration claim in MVA or a new candidate rank.
[Chang et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40470792/)

The useful postnatal research objective remains reducing ongoing damage while
preserving tissue function and limiting abnormal-clone protection. The next
commitment is the qualified exact-genotype/correction comparison, followed
by a mechanism-matched exposure experiment. More protein, alignment or
survival alone cannot earn that commitment. No new wet experiment or clinical
benefit has been demonstrated.
